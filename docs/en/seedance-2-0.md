## Choose one job for the video

A short product video works best when every shot supports the same customer promise.

| Video job | Customer should understand | Evidence the video must protect |
|---|---|---|
| Product showcase | What the product looks like | Shape, color, material, controls, logo, package details |
| Feature demonstration | What one visible feature does | Correct component, physical action, start state, end state |
| Product-page hero video | Why the product deserves attention | Recognizable product, clear hierarchy, quiet copy-safe area |
| Advertising short | What single message should be remembered | Approved claim, relevant visual proof, recognizable closing product |

If the brief contains several unrelated promises, split it into a series. Each video can then be reviewed against one purpose instead of several competing ideas.

## Build a product evidence pack

Prepare these materials before writing the shot:

1. **Product facts:** category, dimensions or proportions, color, material, components, controls, accessories, and package arrangement;
2. **Approved brand details:** logo position, label position, package copy, approved colors, and prohibited visual treatments;
3. **Visual references:** current product photography and approved campaign assets;
4. **Claim evidence:** the reviewed source for any performance, material, or use-case statement;
5. **Release rules:** destination, aspect ratio, copy-safe area, AI-content labeling, and asset-rights checks.

Use the physical product, approved photography, packaging files, or a reviewed specification sheet as the source of truth. Style language may change the mood; it must not change verified product facts.

## Write a three-beat shot plan

### Opening: establish the product

Show a recognizable product shape and a clear environment. Avoid an opening effect that hides the item the customer is meant to evaluate.

### Proof: show one visible action

Use one camera move, product action, or lighting change to carry the customer promise. A single observable event is easier to review than several simultaneous transformations.

### Close: return to a usable product frame

Finish with the complete product, stable composition, and any required copy-safe area. The closing frame should still work when viewed without the preceding motion.

## Five-step Seedance 2.0 workflow

### 1. Open the official model page

Start from the [Seedance 2.0 page on Flux Art](https://flux-art.cc/en/models/seedance-2-0). Confirm the video job, the approved product evidence, and the destination before submitting the first shot.

### 2. Lock the product facts

Write a short fixed-facts list covering the product structure, material, color, branding, and required components. Put these facts before the creative direction so they remain the basis for review.

### 3. Describe one shot in a stable order

Use this order: purpose, product facts, environment, opening frame, one visible action, camera behavior, lighting, closing frame, required details, and prohibited additions. Concrete observations are more useful than broad requests such as “make it viral” or “make it premium.”

### 4. Review in layers

Check product identity first, then the physical action, camera motion, light, background, and closing composition. Correct one problem class at a time. If the product structure is wrong, do not spend the next revision polishing the atmosphere.

### 5. Complete the release check

Verify product claims, logo and package copy, reference-asset rights, AI-content labeling, and the destination platform's current requirements. Use the [compliance checklist](../06-compliance.md) before publishing.

## Two prompts to adapt

### Skincare bottle material reveal

```text
Create an ecommerce product showcase for one cylindrical frosted-white skincare bottle with a pale-gold pump. Keep the bottle proportions, pump structure, dark-gray front logo, label position, material, and color unchanged. Open on the complete bottle standing on a warm light-gray studio surface. Move the camera slowly from a slight front angle toward the label while a soft highlight travels across the frosted surface. End on the complete bottle in a centered, stable composition with clean copy space on the upper right. Use one natural contact shadow. Do not add flowers, liquid splashes, extra text, accessories, hands, or a second product.
```

### Headphones comfort advertising short

```text
Create a focused advertising short for one matte-black over-ear headset. Keep the ear-cup shape, headband structure, side mark, button positions, material, and color unchanged. Open with the complete headset on a clean walnut desk. Make one smooth camera move from the ear cushion detail back to the full product while soft window light from the left reveals the cushion texture. End with the complete headset at a slight front angle and quiet negative space on the left. Do not add people, screens, glowing parts, extra controls, brand text, or another headset.
```

Replace the product facts and customer promise with information from your own approved source materials. Keep one primary action in each shot.

## Review and correction table

| Problem | Check first | Next correction |
|---|---|---|
| Product changes during motion | Structure, component count, controls, logo, label | Restate the fixed product facts and simplify the action |
| Motion looks physically unclear | Start state, end state, contact points | Describe one observable action with a defined result |
| Camera move hides the product | Product scale, angle, crop, path | Shorten the move and return to a recognizable full-product view |
| Background competes with the message | Prop count, contrast, unrelated movement | Remove secondary actions and reduce background contrast |
| Customer promise is not visible | Shot purpose and proof beat | Rewrite the proof as one action the viewer can observe |
| Closing frame is unusable | Product completeness, stability, copy-safe area | Specify a stable complete-product ending |

**Q: How should I structure an ecommerce product video?**

Use a three-beat plan: establish the product, show one visible action that supports the customer promise, and close on a complete, stable product frame. Keep all three beats tied to the same purpose.

**Q: How can I reduce product drift during a shot?**

Create a fixed product-facts list, use one primary action, and review structure before camera, lighting, or atmosphere. When the product changes, simplify the shot and correct only the identity problem first.

**Q: How do I choose between Seedance 2.0, Grok Video, and HappyHorse 1.1?**
